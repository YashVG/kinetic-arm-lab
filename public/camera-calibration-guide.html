Calibrate your webcam

Estimate lens distortion with OpenCV, then compare the original and corrected camera feed in Kinetic. Your images stay local.

1. Prepare a checkerboard

Download the 9 × 6 inner-corner checkerboard. Print it at 100% on landscape paper, measure the squares, and mount it flat. The pattern has 10 × 7 squares. Default square size is 25 mm.

2. Set up the local utility

In the Kinetic project folder, use Python 3.11 or newer:

python3 -m venv .venv-calibration
.venv-calibration/bin/python -m pip install -r tools/calibration/requirements.txt

3. Capture varied views

Turn off the website camera first. Keep the camera mode, zoom and focus fixed. Use the same webcam for capture and the website.

.venv-calibration/bin/python tools/calibration/calibrate.py --camera 0 --columns 9 --rows 6 --square-mm 25 --width 640 --height 480

Move and tilt the board across the center, edges and corners at different distances. Keep the whole pattern sharp and visible. Press Space to save a detected view, Enter to solve, or Esc to cancel. Capture 20 distinct views; at least 12 are required. Use a new --output folder for each run.

4. Review and import

Review outputs/camera-calibration/comparison.jpg and the per-view errors in camera-profile.json. In Kinetic, expand Lens correction and import that JSON. Enable the camera again, calibrate your neutral pose, then engage.

The profile stays in this tab's memory. The operator view and MediaPipe use the corrected image; the comparison below shows the original. Disabling correction stops the camera and clears neutral calibration.

What the numbers mean

Training RMS is the root-mean-square corner reprojection error in pixels. Every fifth view is held out from intrinsic calibration; its board pose is fitted separately to report held-out RMS. Inspect both errors and image coverage. Repeated views or blurred patterns can yield a poor profile despite a low error.

Correction time is the CPU remap cost per frame. Inference p95 excludes remapping; frame age includes it. Use the same scene and motion with correction on and off to compare latency, visible straightness and tracking stability. This does not establish metric depth accuracy or guarantee better MediaPipe tracking.

Only OpenCV's five-coefficient pinhole model is supported. Fisheye profiles are rejected. Exact resolution is required; no crop or scaling is applied. Black borders can appear. A camera change, zoom change or focus change requires a new profile.

Method: OpenCV camera calibration. The Python utility performs calibration; the browser applies its distortion model using a tested pixel remap.
